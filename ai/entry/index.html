Method and assumptions

Three models are trained on past data. The model with the lowest validation error is selected and used for the test strategy.

  1. Chronological split: 60% training, 20% validation and 20% testing. Labels overlapping the boundaries are purged.
  2. Target return: next session open to the close at session 21 or 63. The simulation includes entry and exit costs.
  3. One position at a time, exiting at the end of the horizon. No leverage or short selling. Cash earns zero return.
  4. Simulated entry: predicted return exceeds total costs plus 0.5 percentage points. This threshold is a fixed design assumption.
Models, assumptions and limitations

Ridge penalty: 0.1. Gradient boosting: 70 decision stumps, learning rate 0.06. Neural network: one layer of 8 tanh units with L2 regularization. Features: 5-, 20- and 60-session returns, 20- and 60-session volatility, relative volume, daily range, intraday return, overnight gap and distance from the 20-session mean.

This research implementation adapts model families discussed by Gu, Kelly and Xiu; it does not reproduce the paper. A broad company panel, fundamentals, CNN and a general pretrained model are not implemented.

Passing the initial screen requires positive forecast improvement, positive test return above buy and hold, at least 12 trades, and horizon volatility no higher than 15%. These are design rules, not statistical significance tests or guarantees of future performance.

Volatility uses square-root-of-time scaling, assuming stable volatility and no autocorrelation. Overlapping returns mean forecast counts are not independent observation counts. Adjusted prices produce adjusted returns; taxes and account-currency risk are excluded.

For the latest forecast, the selected model is refitted using all samples with completed returns. Historical testing uses the fixed model fitted before the test period. Changing settings after viewing the test makes that period development data.
